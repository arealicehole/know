## summary

AZRSOL is an Arizona 501(c)(3) all-volunteer nonprofit (EIN 82-2527065), NARSOL state affiliate, founded by **Patricia (Pat) and Terry Borden** after their son **Stephen May** received a 75-year sentence they describe as wrongful. Public brand line: **education, advocacy, and support** for people on Arizona’s sexual offense registry and their loved ones; tagline frame **“Supporting Public Safety, Human Dignity, and Successful Rehabilitation.”**

Stated advocacy goals (official site + fundraising copy) include: law-enforcement-only registry access; end registry after court-imposed sentence; reverse retroactive restrictions; reform civil commitment; rehumanize/reintegrate/rehabilitate; increase public safety by reducing sexual offenses; reduce discrimination/hatred/violence against people with sexual offense convictions. They explicitly state they **support victim rights**, that sexual harm is serious, and that reform is not excusing harm. They are **not registered lobbyists** but meet policymakers, encourage Arizona Request-to-Speak (RTS), and are fundraising around challenges to **SB1404 / SB1236** and related litigation (**Doe et al. v. Mayes et al.**, Case 2:24-cv-02259-MTL — they report not granted plaintiff status but remain involved / possible amicus).

Public leadership names found: founders **Terry & Patricia Borden** (Executive Directors in conference materials); **Vicky Campo** (Communications / Marketing Coordinator); **John Covert** (Executive Committee; Arizona Capitol Times / NARSOL op-ed); **Penny@azrsol.org** (RTS help). Full board roster is **not** published on the main site.

Confidence overall: **high** for mission/positions/voice from official site; **medium** for full officer list and live session bill matrix (needs Johnny + current AZLeg scrape).

## 3. Voice / never-say notes

### Voice samples (official tone)

1. **Institutional calm + moral pairing:** “Supporting Public Safety, Human Dignity, and Successful Rehabilitation.”  
2. **Evidence vs fear:** “provide research alongside lived experience… reduce stigma and dissuade fear-based laws and policies.”  
3. **Victim-inclusive bridge:** “We believe sexual harm is serious. Victims deserve compassion… Supporting reform does not mean excusing harm… We are two sides of the same coin.”  
4. **Rights universalizer:** “…because denying any group of citizens their civil, constitutional, or human rights threatens the essential liberties of us all.”  
5. **Lived-experience authority:** “All of our volunteers are either directly-impacted or have loved ones who are.”  
6. **Person-first / registry language:** prefers “those living on the sexual offense registry,” “registered citizens,” “people who have committed sexual offenses” over slurs; Fearless copy uses “registered citizens and their adult family members.”  
7. **Empowerment register:** survivors, resilient, tenacious, problem-solvers; “develop their voices and become self-reliant.”  
8. **Covert op-ed register (public intellectual):** academic studies, ALI MPC, DOJ 3.5% reconviction figure, Ginsburg dissent quote — policy-wonk, not rage-bait.  
9. **Stephen May page register:** more prosecutorial-injustice rhetoric (“mockery of justice,” “crime that never happened”) — **case-specific**; do not generalize that tone to all AZRSOL policy mail without approval.

### Recommended drafting defaults for Azimuth (derived, labeled)

| Do (grounded in public voice) | Don’t (inferred risk — confirm with Johnny) |
|---|---|
| Lead with public safety + fewer victims + evidence | Lead with pure offender-sympathy without victim sentence |
| Say “people on the registry” / “registered citizens” | Casual use of “predator,” “monster,” “pedophile” as blanket labels for all registrants |
| Cite research / ALI / lived experience together | Claim AZRSOL “doesn’t care about victims” or “wants to abolish accountability” |
| Distinguish LE-only registry vs public shaming website | Imply AZRSOL wants zero consequences or no supervision ever (they attack *public* lifetime collateral regimes; civil commitment *reform*, not necessarily “release all now” slogan) |
| Note they are not lobbyists but educate officials | Call them a PAC or lobby shop |
| For SB1404/SB1236: “worsening restrictions,” legal challenge | Invent vote counts or cosponsor attacks not on site |
| Pair Stephen May content with clear “this is a specific case AZRSOL highlights” | Treat every registrant story as identical to May’s innocence claim |
| Perspectives blog disclaimer exists: OTheR posts “do not necessarily reflect the opinions of AZRSOL” | Treat every Perspectives/OTheR post as binding org policy |

### “Never-say” candidates (org-sensitive — **Johnny must confirm**)

Public site does **not** publish a formal banned-words list. For Phase A safety, treat as **provisional never-say in AZRSOL voice** until Johnny signs off:

- Framing reform as anti-victim or anti-child-safety.  
- Jokes, gore, or vigilante rhetoric.  
- Speaking *as* AZRSOL on a bill not briefed.  
- Conflating NARSOL national positions with AZ-only priorities without check.  
- Using “sex offender” as the org’s preferred default noun in headlines if site prefers “sexual offense” / “registry” constructions (site still uses “sex offender” in some linked titles/tools — mixed; prefer person-first in new drafts).  
- Claiming plaintiff status in Doe v. Mayes (they said they were **not** granted it).  
- Promising legal advice or endorsing specific attorneys/housing vendors (reentry page: informational only, no endorsement).
