# Restaurant Local Marketing Bible — Independent 100% Halal Mediterranean Grill / Fast-Casual
## Chandler, AZ (East Valley) — Google/GBP-first, ratings-protection, capacity-constrained

**Prepared for:** Jim Bob (client research / resources/verticals)
**Date:** 2026-11-26
**Scope:** One independent 100% halal Mediterranean grill/fast-casual in Chandler AZ. 2–3 floor staff. GBP-first discovery. Ratings protection > viral growth.
**Status:** Draft playbook. No invented statistics. Where a number is a judgment call, it is labeled "heuristic / to-verify". Not affiliated with any operator.

---

## 0. Objective
Deliver a practical, week-one-ready local marketing system for a capacity-constrained, single-location independent halal Mediterranean grill in Chandler AZ. The system is built around one operating philosophy: **Google/GBP-first discovery + ratings protection over viral growth.** Social media is explicitly frozen then reactivated at 3–5 posts/week.

---

## 1. Channel stack and weekly operating rhythm

### Channel stack (priority order, non-negotiable)
| Tier | Channel | Role | Why first / why frozen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google Business Profile (GBP) | Primary discovery surface. Reviews, photos, posts/specials, attributes | Google Maps organic is the default path East Valley diners use; GBP is the only channel with a "specials" surface. |
| 2 | Google Search (on-site SEO) | Semi-permanent. Name, address, hours, menu via schema, halal attribute, East Valley + Chandler + halal keywords | Supports GBP; do not over-invest here. |
| 3 | Email + SMS (list) | List retention and direct marketing. Both need opt-in. | Cheap, compounding, but spam-sensitive. |
| 4 | Paid (GBP/local search ads) | Only after organic + GBP hygiene are stable. Small, recency-only budgets. | No proof of ROI yet; don't scale. |
| 5 | Social (frozen Phase 1; reactivate Phase 2+) | Reactivated at 3–5 posts/week. Instagram + Facebook; TikTok optional/low priority. No 6-figure time cost. | Mandate: "social currently frozen then 3-5 posts/week reactivation." |
| 6 | Marketplace listings (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub) | Delivery/pickup presence. Second-order discovery. Not the primary channel. | Highest commission, lowest margin, NAP-sensitive. |
| 7 | Third-party sites (Yelp, Tripadvisor, Zomato) | Defend presence; do not chase. Keep every listing consistent. | Do not chase; costly to drive from. |

### Weekly operating rhythm (Phase 1, frozen social)
- **Mon** — GBP + marketplace audit (NAP, hours, category, photos). Check reviews for the week; reply to all new reviews (target: 2 business days).
- **Tue** — Prep-list review for Fri–Sat specials. Verify POS/menu lock against the specials calendar.
- **Wed** — GBP posts/specials — 1 post (the Fri–Sat special anchor). Internal check on the review ask machine (trigger firing, opt-outs honored).
- **Thu** — Email/SMS send or confirm scheduled (max 2–4 texts/mo per policy; weekly batch minimum).
- **Fri** — Special launch. Photo + GBP post. Track sell-through vs. prep.
- **Sat** — Special live. Watch capacity; do not push more specials than 2–3 items.
- **Sun** — Review/listening pass. Monday prep: set next week's special + confirm marketplace photo hygiene.

### Phase 2 (week 5+) — social reactivation
3–5 posts/week across Instagram + Facebook. Rotation: 1 hero dish (2×/wk), 1 "behind the scenes / craft" (1×/wk), 1 halal/local culture (1×/wk, careful — do not anthropomorphize a religious identity), 1 special/weekend (weekend-aligned), 1 founder/owner voice note optional. Never more than 2 in one day. One hero asset repurposed across all platforms (see section 5.5). No more than one "special" photo per week — that's what is the special.

**Hard rule:** social visibility does not replace GBP; GBP is the source of truth.

## 2. Review-ask machine

### Principles
- **Ask everyone a completed experience got.** Never gate the public review on a private survey result (FTC / platform policies).
- **One ask per guest per 90 days.** Frequency cap.
- **Single reminder max, then stop.**
- **No incentives tied to positive sentiment.** Incentives allowed only for neutral opt-in participation, never "give us 5 stars."
- **Neutral wording.** "Share your experience honestly." No star rating selection before the Google link.

### Trigger
- Trigger event = the **completion of the experience** (bill settled for restaurant; for takeout, pick-up confirmed).
- Timing heuristics (to verify locally): weekday lunch — same day mid-afternoon; weekday dinner — next morning ~10:00; weekend brunch — early evening. SMS within 24h; email 48–72h if no response.

### Channel stack for the ask
| Channel | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SMS (opt-in) | Within hours of bill/pickup | Primary. SMS converts 3–6× email (vendor guide). Under 300 chars. |
| Email | 48–72h if no SMS click | Cleanup channel. |
| QR card at table | Always on | For walk-ins without contact. |
| Staff verbal ask at payment | At payment | Optional, no permission required. |

### Message (short, three rules)
- Neutral, single ask, single link, one reminder.
- SMS template (adapt): `Hi [Name], thanks for dining with us at [Brand]. Mind leaving an honest Google review? [Link]. Reply STOP to opt out.`
- Email subject: `How was your meal, [First]?` Body: thanks + one link + private feedback link + unsubscribe.

### Suppression logic
- Do not send to a number that has replied STOP, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel, unsubscribe (regardless of time).
- No duplicate sends within 90 days per guest.
- After confirmed completion or opt-out → suppress.
- Auditable log: eligibility, consent, message version, destination, opt-outs.

### Expected KPIs (Phase 1)
- 30–50% invite-to-click (heuristic; vendor-reported SMS click-through 15–25%).
- Review conversion: 3–6% of invited guests; 5–12% depending on channel (vendor-claimed; verify locally).
- Opt-out rate < 2%.
- All reviews replied to within 2 business days.

## 3. SMS / list retention (without spam)

### Consent / compliance (US, TCPA/CTIA/CAN-SPAM)
- Prior express written consent before any marketing text. Checkbox, not pre-checked. Names number, business, frequency, opt-out, fees, privacy link.
- Confirmation text after signup: program name, frequency, help contact, how to stop.
- Every message: opt-out line ("Reply STOP to opt out"). Honor within FCC timeline (≤10 business days; do not push it to the limit — honor immediately).
- Frequency cap: max 2–4 marketing texts/month. "We miss you" win-back 30–45 days idle.
- Never buy/rent/share lists.
- Records: timestamps, language shown, IP, opt-in method, retention ≥4 years.
- Do not send texts 8am–9pm window (local).

### List building
- Checkout checkbox (separate, unchecked).
- Table tents with keyword JOIN.
- QR codes on receipts/bags.
- Website footer signup.
- Optional incentive: small one-time discount or early access to limited-time offers (acceptable; not "review incentive").

### List hygiene
- Merge contacts by phone/email matched at first opt-in.
- No stale lists; winback at 30–45 days.
- Never reuse order-confirmation contact info for marketing without separate opt-in.

## 4. Lunch habit systems (plaza lunch club)

### Model
Plaza-style weekly lunch club. Anchor weekday lunch traffic into repeat visitors.
- **Weekday lunch club:** pre-booked weekly lunch seats (6–12 guests per seat, capped at kitchen capacity). 2–3 anchor restaurants, 1 item per club. Target 8–14 confirmed seats per week.
- **"Lunch Club Friday" / rotating weekly dish:** Monday set, Friday launches.
- **Office-friendly catering / pre-packaged boxes** for small Chandler East Valley offices.
- **Guest pass system:** new → $0-$10 signup fee → unlock a seat + first discount → return.

### Operational rules
- Never cap seats below kitchen capacity; cap at 1.2× demand.
- Only pre-sold seats; no "open lunch" that competes with the club.
- Never promise exact seat times outside the club.
- One seat offer per office/club, not per day.
- No viral/marketing push for the club; drive by direct outreach + GBP post.

## 5. WEEKEND SPECIALS system design (core deliverable)

### 5.1 System overview
- **Scope:** 2–3 special items max per weekend (1 lunch, 1 dinner, 1 seasonal/limited). Rotating weekly or 4-week cycle. No more than ~15–25% of the menu at any time (rotational layer heuristic).
- **Lock-in cadence:** Saturday night close → Sunday prep lock → Monday–Tuesday scheduling → Friday launch.
- **Prep windows:** Monday–Wednesday prep; Thursday freeze; Friday soft launch; Saturday sell-through.

### 5.2 Prep list gate
- **Gate 1 — Friday 10:00 AM (Monday prep opens):** run a prep list against a 1.2× expected weekend demand. Do not prep beyond forecast.
- **Gate 2 — Thursday 10:00 AM (freeze):** no new items added. Senior line cook signs the prep sheet.
- **Gate 3 — Friday launch (9:00 AM):** POS/menu lock. No specials edited after lock. Only price or sold-out edits.

### 5.3 Pricing framework
- Cost per item target 30–35% food cost (industry average for full-service; fast-casual target 28–32%).
- **Markup formula:** menu price = food cost / target food-cost %.
- Starter pricing heuristic: use a 2.8–3.2× markup on comparable core items as the ceiling. Specials: do not go below core list price. No "promo price loss leaders".
- Hard rule: no special at a loss. No "cost-leadership" weekend special.

### 5.4 POS/menu lock gates
- Weekly menu change must be reconciled to POS by Thursday night.
- Sync GBP posts/specials, marketplace listings, and Instagram to a single source of truth (a "weekend special source file" — single doc, version-stamped).
- Marketplace listing updates (DoorDash/Uber Eats/Grubhub) must follow same lock; check 48h before launch.
- **Sold-out priority:** specials sold out get "sold out" state in POS + GBP + email/SMS cancel ("We're out"). Do not let the next-day queue flood the kitchen with failed specials.

### 5.5 Creative / photo workflow
- **One hero shot, repurposed.** 1 dish per special = 1 hero image = 6–12 assets (Instagram grid, FB post, GBP post, marketing email header, marketplace hero).
- Photoshoot session: 30–45 min for 2–3 specials before lunch service. One static dish + one detail shot + one table shot. Phone + iOS editing is sufficient.
- Refresh cadence: one new hero weekly (special), not more. Use existing assets for Non-special posts.
- Never use AI-generated food on GBP; GBP and social photo policies require real food. Keep it honest.
- Photo workflow: shoot Friday AM → crop 4:5, 1:1, 9:16 → publish Friday post → marketplace image updated Saturday.

### 5.6 When NOT to run specials
- Not before planned staff loss (e.g. open house week).
- Not on slow days (weekday lunch should stay core).
- Not on dishes that require one-off ingredients new to supplier — run one-off only in trial mode with pre-buys.
- Not on specials that create a "margin loss" (below 35% food cost).
- Not on anything that requires new equipment or new décor.
- Not on one-off cultural dishes that never launch — a retired Tepsi-style dish (if real, retired for capacity) stays retired. Do not revive for a weekend special unless you have capacity + believable review demand.

### 5.7 Alternatives to one-off cultural dishes that never launch
1. **Repetition-based specials:** take a proven core item (al-martabak, kebab, tabbouleh) and reframe: "Friday family-style platter", "Saturday lunch box", "Sunday breakfast favorites". Same kitchen, new packaging.
2. **Bundles and family packs:** 2-plate family pack, 3-meal weekly lunch bundle — same item, higher ticket, lower prep complexity.
3. **Seasonal specials only:** 4-week rotation, with 2 fixed (core lunch) + 1 rotating (seasonal) + 1 "preview".
4. **Takeout/delivery-only items:** items that ship well (shawarma box, rice bowl). No fresh garnish needed; margin-richer.
5. **Office pre-orders:** custom lunch boxes for Chandler offices (weekday lunch club), not weekend.

### 5.8 Specials compliance gate
- Halal certification must remain verifiable per special (paid halal certificate not required to list "halal" but must not mislead).
- Pricing displayed at launch must match the pre-launch list. No mid-service surprise price changes.
- If special sold out at 1 pm, GBP post + email/SMS must mark sold out by 2 pm.
- Record special sell-through, food cost, margin weekly.

## 6. Marketplace hygiene (DoorDash / Uber Eats / Grubhub)

### NAP consistency
- Chandler, AZ address across Google, Yelp, Apple Maps, Foursquare, all marketplaces must match exactly. No abbreviated address variation.
- Phone, hours, menu unchanged across all listings.
- Menu URLs — marketplace menu mirror should match the core menu, not the weekend special (specials vary by day). Use a separate "specials" sheet if the marketplace supports daily add-ons; otherwise remove specials from marketplace listings entirely (delivery-only margin trap).
- Category: "Restaurants" / "Mediterranean" / "Middle Eastern" / "Grill" depending on marketplace. Pick the most specific, not the highest-volume keyword. List halal within description.
- Images: 1–3 marketplace photos from a clean shoot; no stock.
- Delivery fees / promotions: do not rely on marketplace commissions; use marketplace as discovery, not revenue.
- In-app ratings: reply to all reviews inside 48h. Same as GBP.

### Do NOT
- Use marketplace as the ONLY discovery channel.
- List the special price online before it's truly a special (no "% off" lies).
- Add "halal" or "American" without confirming. Certified halal cert does not bypass halal marketing rules.

## 7. Bio / positioning language (healthy, not fad identity)

### Healthy identity (defensible, non-virtue-signaling)
- "Independent 100% halal Mediterranean grill in Chandler, AZ. Family-style, generous, no fluff."
- Values: Honest food, halal cert/QA, East Valley neighborhood, local Chandler pride.
- Tone: Family-owned, no "perfect", no "100% clean diet", no keto/vegan/gluten-free-by-identity.
- Halal positioning: "100% halal, by family standard, never by marketing trick" — honest certification status displayed (e.g. "Halal certified" or "Halal prepared on-site"). Never imply certifications you don't have.
- Avoid: "clean eating", "wellness", "vegan-friendly on request", "paleo/keto", "no-GMO", "gluten-free". Do not lean on health identity.
- Avoid: "moroccan"/"turkish" if the family isn't — halal does not equal one nationality. Mediterranean grill is the honest banner.
- Avoid: "100% halal & healthy". Halal and healthy are separate claims; only claim halal if certified, only imply health if true.

### Bio copy template
- Tagline: "Family-style Mediterranean grill — 100% halal, Chandler, AZ."
- Bio: "Independent halal Mediterranean grill rooted in the Chandler East Valley. Family-owned, 2–3 floor staff, 6–8 day-part menu. Grab-and-go lunch club + weekend specials."
- GBP description (short): "100% halal family-style Mediterranean grill. Fresh kebab, tabbouleh, rice bowls. Chandler, AZ."
- Social bio (Instagram/FB first-line): "100% halal Mediterranean grill. Chandler, AZ."

## 8. 90-day phased plan

### Phase 1 (Days 1–30) — Foundation + GBP first
- GBP listing complete and verified; all attributes, photos, and description set.
- Review-ask machine live (SMS/email/QR).
- Marketplaces updated to core menu (no specials).
- Social frozen.
- 1% conservative: 2–3 posts/week max if any (GBP only).
- **KPI: 2–3 new Google reviews per week with reply rate ≥95%; GBP listed as verified.**

### Phase 2 (Days 31–60) — Soft launch + repeat traffic
- Weekend specials system live (Saturday-launch, 2–3 items).
- Social reactivated at 3 posts/week.
- Lunch club seats (10–14 weekly).
- Win-back SMS flow (30–45 days).
- **KPI: 10–14 lunch club seats per week + 30–40% new-visit retention (week-over-week repeat).**

### Phase 3 (Days 61–90) — Optimization + discipline
- 5 posts/week social.
- Full weekend specials rhythm.
- Marketplace listings audited (NAP unchanged).
- GBP posts scheduled (2/week).
- **KPI: steady 10+ new Google reviews/month, 0% stale marketplace listing, 2–4 social posts per week but only if reviews are protected.**

### Guardrails across all phases
- No outrushed staff — 2–3 floor staff cannot carry more than 5 posts/week + 3–5 specials + 1 lunch club. If anything slips, cut.

## 9. Claim risks and must-nots

### Must-nots (claim risks)
- Do NOT claim "100% halal" or "100% kosher" if not certified/verified. "Halal prepared on-site" if not certified. No vague "halal-ish" marketing.
- Do NOT claim "healthier" or "clean" without evidence.
- Do NOT claim "authentic"/"family recipes" for non-authentic dishes.
- Do NOT claim "fastest" / "best" / "award-winning" — only opinion.
- Do NOT claim "locally owned" unless actually owned.
- Do NOT list "vegetarian" / "vegan" / "gluten-free" on GBP if not certified or verbally confirmed.
- Do NOT use before/after photos or "healing" claims.
- Do NOT incentivize reviews (FTC).
- Do NOT buy reviews, followers, or likes.
- Do NOT send unsolicited texts.
- Do NOT cross the line on blunt "halal"/"kosher" identity claims in markets where it is not certified.

### FTC/compliance must-nots
- No unsubstantiated food health claims.
- No paid "5-star" review services.
- No review gating or private-only routing.
- No disclaimers on results unless accurate.
- No false "certified" badges.

### Reputational must-nots
- Replying to bad reviews with sarcasm; dismissive public replies.
- Reviewing only the good ones; "let us know if you had a bad experience" without a path to private feedback.
- Over-promise on specials (sold-out next day).
- Wrong addresses on marketplace (NAP mismatch).
